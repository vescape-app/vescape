# The map camera is one critically damped spring engine that shadow-tracks gestures and skips invisible writes

- **Closed-form critically damped springs per axis:** each axis integrates exactly for any `dt`, and a retarget keeps both position and velocity, so motion stays continuous however often the Map Camera Controller changes its target. Critical damping never reverses, so `release()` coasts each axis to `x + v/ω` and the camera glides to a stop instead of bouncing.
- **Skip writes a rider cannot see:** in compass mode the heading spring is retargeted by a 60 Hz sensor and never settles, so without this filter camera writes run for the whole ride. Each write fires `onCameraChanged` synchronously and commits a Core Animation transform for the compass ornament, and that load exhausts the scene-update watchdog. Thresholds sit just under one pixel: about 11 cm of centre, and a rotation that moves the edge of a phone-wide viewport by well under a pixel. Skipping drops whole frames instead of making each one cheaper.
- **Gestures are shadow-tracked:** while a finger or native momentum moves the camera, `driveExternal` copies the position into the springs and derives velocity from it without writing the camera. The next target then blends out of the gesture with no jump. The caller's `gesture` flag is the only filter it needs, because the engine can identify echoes of its own writes accurately and the map callback cannot.
- **Echoes are not drivers:** non-gesture samples arriving within `echoWindowMs` of a write are discarded. Two echoes on either side of one write would differentiate into the engine's own jump. A stale echo also moves the springs backwards, so the teleport check measures from the wrong place, and the same A→B move sometimes snaps and sometimes crawls. A sample further than `maxDriveJumpPx` away is treated as a reposition and starts a fresh drive. Samples closer than `MIN_DRIVE_DT_S` are coalesced, because a stalled JS thread drains queued touches in sub-millisecond bursts.
- **An app-issued target owns the camera until it lands:** while `targetOwned` is set, non-gesture samples are ignored, because a fling that started before the target keeps reporting its own deceleration. Only a finger clears it.
- **Fingers drive and springs follow:** a pinch writes the camera directly instead of retargeting a spring, because a spring target trails the fingers by its time constant and the pinch feels sluggish. When a pinch ends, zoom is pinned where the fingers left it, and a released drag does not coast pitch, which is derived from zoom. Pitch-follows-zoom never writes while the engine is animating, or the two writers vibrate against each other.
- **Long moves snap or arc:** a centre retarget beyond `teleportDistanceM` (50 km) snaps. A shorter jump caps the zoom target until the remaining distance fits about `fitPx` (320 px) on screen, then releases it, so the camera arcs out and back.
